Why Is Your Breath Hot and Cold?

▶ Watch the Short

The answer

A thin, fast "hoo" jet drags in room air on the way, so it arrives cool and pulls heat off your skin. A slow "haa" barely mixes and lands warm.

Sources (3)

  1. Robert Matthews (physicist), BBC Science Focus, "Why does blowing hard on our hands cool them, but just breathing on them warm them?": it is "essentially due to a difference in the speed of the airflow"; slow breath has time to pass its warmth and moisture to the hand; fast pursed-lip air blasts away warmed air, and the fast column "draws in, or 'entrains', the air around it", which is cooler. https://www.sciencefocus.com/science/why-does-blowing-hard-on-our-hands-cool-them-but-just-breathing-on-them-warm-them
  2. The Naked Scientists (Cambridge), "Why is your breath sometimes warm and sometimes cold": at ~15 cm the pursed-lip jet has entrained mostly room air, which removes heat from the hand by conduction and evaporation; right at the lips the same jet still feels warm (so the breath itself starts warm). https://www.thenakedscientists.com/get-naked/experiments/why-your-breath-sometimes-warm-and-sometimes-cold
  3. Turbulent jets entrain surrounding air, and the entrained mass grows with distance: Ricou & Spalding (1961), "Measurements of entrainment by axisymmetrical turbulent jets", J. Fluid Mech. 11:21–32. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022112061000834
